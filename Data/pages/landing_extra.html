Stolen Timelines
Stolen Timelines

Stolen Timelines is a fast-paced platformer, where you play as a thief from the future, stealing artefacts from the past in order to meet the gang quota.

I was responsible for critical elements such as the gameplay camera: a dynamic camera that would adapt to the player's movement and the terrain to offer a clear view of the environment without hindering the gameplay experience by utilising: directional biases; interpolation; camera bounds along with fine tuning from testing and feedback.

I also designed gameplay mechanics, a notable one being the rewind ability, allowing the player to avoid fatal traps or save time while backtracking. On ability activation the player would place down a beacon to save their coordinates and current time remaining, if the player then activated the ability again, they would return to that position and in-game time.
I was primarily responsible for asset implantation within the game, taking character sprite sheets or .wav audio files and implementing them in engine. I would then work with respective artists or audio designers to tweak and fine tune their assets.

Bar Fight
Bar Fight

This project was completed during the Global Game Jam 2024.

Responsible to the mechanics design and programming, the notable mechanics I worked on were AI and the ragdoll physics.

The AI was created using a pathfinding algorithm and a state machine to allow the enemy to chase the player once they entered their vision cone, utilising a Navmesh to allow the enemy to move around the level whilst avoiding obstacels.

Nearly all objects in the game were interactable, allowing the player to throw objects at the enemy to ragdoll them. The enemies would then be given an impulse force to knock their ragdoll back. This helped in creating responsive gameplay and utilises ragdoll physics to create a humorous and engaging experience.

Post-Poned
Post-Poned

Post-Poned is a light-hearted, 3D puzzle game where you play as Keith, the postman.

I was responsible for the level design, gameplay camera and the puzzle mechanics.

The camera was a unique as it was from a fixed perspective with a twist: multiple cameras were available to the player, and they could switch between them willingly. Every camera could be moved independently to reveal points of interest to the player encouraging the player to explore with the camera as well as the player character.

Each puzzle was unique, allowing the player to exercise their problem-solving skills throughout the game. The conveyor puzzle was compromised of parcels that would spawn in sequence, each needing to be scanned by the player. Each object needed the player to press buttons corresponding to the shape, size and contents of the object which were hinted at by the parcel models and the voice lines explaining the contents.

Networking with FishNet
Networking with FishNet

This project explored how games manage online play, focusing on which network architecture is best for small-scale games and development and which API best fits the network type.
The outcome consists of a basic, playable prototype allowing players to host or join a server.

Players join a host's server by entering the host's join code. The host communicates with the clients through a relay, bypassing the need to port forward.

Within the game the players can move and shoot at other players, with the host synchronising player and projectile positions for all clients through the use of client and server RPCs.
